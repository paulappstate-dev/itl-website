Foundations

Three questions, taught like methods.

Three questions run through every course. They look simple. They aren't. The program is built to make them answerable on your own ground — not in a textbook, not on a slide, but in the conditions you actually live in.

01
Cultural Intelligence (CQ)

How do you
read across cultures?

The research-backed answer is Cultural Intelligence — CQ — a four-capability framework used in business, government, and NGOs. You don't memorize it; you run it on your own work, your own teams, your own assumptions.

  • Drivemotivate
  • Knowledgeobserve
  • Strategyplan
  • Actionadapt
02
Adaptive Leadership

How do you
lead through ambiguity?

The discipline of moving forward when the path is unclear — when the problem has no precedent and the team has no playbook. You learn to distinguish technical problems from adaptive ones, and to lead the latter without faking the former.

  • Adaptive problemsdiagnose
  • Root causesname
  • Experimentsrun
  • Changemove
03
Distributed Practice

How do you
work across distance?

Distance is the modern default — time zones, screens, languages, partial overlaps. Working well across it is its own practice: building trust with people you'll never share a meal with, writing instead of meeting, running decisions that hold up overnight.

  • Team contractsset
  • Async rhythmsrun
  • Decision logskeep
  • Trust at distancebuild

Coursework

Five courses. Fifteen credits.

A short, focused sequence. Take the first four in any order; the practicum comes last. One course per term keeps pace with a working career; you can accelerate if life allows. Most students finish in 12–18 months.

ITL 6100

Cross-Cultural Communication in Global Leadership

Foundations of cross-cultural communication, anchored in the Cultural Intelligence framework. Map your own patterns, run a bias inventory, and leave with a development plan keyed to your work.

3 cr.
ITL 6200

Technology for International Collaborations

How collaboration technology shapes — and distorts — cross-cultural work. Diagnose your team's setup and build a plan for the way you actually work at distance.

3 cr.
ITL 6300

Leadership for Global Challenges

Adaptive leadership for problems that cross borders — climate, health, supply, migration. Pick a real one, diagnose it, propose an intervention that respects culture.

3 cr.
ITL 6400

Leadership for International Teams

Recruit, motivate, and align teams across language and culture. Case-driven and practice-heavy: simulations, conflict work, the patterns that hold under real constraints.

3 cr.
ITL 6500

International Leadership Practicum

Capstone. Pull the work from the previous four courses into one coached, contextual project. Finish the portfolio; sign off the credential.

3 cr.

Who it's for

For people who've outgrown one map.

For leaders whose lives have already widened past one country, one culture, one context — and who want a place to think hard about leading from that vantage.

Your team isn't in one room.

Your colleagues wake hours after you do. What you're building together has to hold across those hours — across screens, distances, the silences in between. You want to learn how to lead a team you'll never share a room with.

01Remote-first companies · Multi-site organizations · Distributed product teams

Your team isn't from one place.

Language, custom, and unspoken assumption sit at the same table as you do. You've already learned a great deal by getting it wrong. You'd like to learn more of it on purpose — and faster.

02Multinationals · NGOs · Hospitals · Educational systems

Your reach isn't one country.

Your students, patients, partners, or constituents live in places you may never visit. The decisions you make reach them anyway. You want a deeper sense of who they are — and how to lead with that responsibility.

03Foreign service · International development · Education abroad · Global trade
Admissions

How to apply.

A short, friendly path. We read for substance, not credentials — tell us how you actually lead and where you want this to go.

The path

  1. Get in touch
    Email the program director with your goals and your context. We'll write back about whether ITL is the right move right now.
  2. Submit application
    App State Graduate School online application: transcripts, current résumé, and a short statement of professional purpose. No GRE.
  3. Two recommendations
    One professional, one academic preferred — but we read for substance, not credentials. Tell us how you actually lead.
  4. Hear back
    Applications are reviewed as they arrive. Apply with enough lead time before your intended start; we'll respond when your file is complete.
Common questions

Asked & answered.

The questions we get most often. Don't see yours here? Reach out — we'd rather have the conversation.

Is travel required?

No. The certificate is fully online and built to fit around the life you already have.

Is travel available?

Yes, on occasion. The program runs short study-abroad opportunities from time to time, and capstone projects can sometimes be sited with an international partner or with your own organization abroad. None of it is required to earn the certificate.

How long does it take?

Most students complete the certificate in 12–18 months at one course per term. You can accelerate or slow down as life requires.

Does this stack into a master's or doctorate?

Yes. Credits earned in the certificate can be applied toward several App State master's programs in related fields, and the certificate can also be taken as a concentration within our interdisciplinary Ed.D.

What does it cost?

App State graduate tuition rates apply; the program is eligible for federal financial aid. Many students are employer-sponsored — we'll help you build the case.

What technology will I need?

A reliable computer, decent webcam and microphone, and broadband internet. We provide access to all coursework platforms and the collaboration stack used in the practicum.
