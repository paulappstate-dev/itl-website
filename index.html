Who it's for

Three kinds of leaders take this certificate.

The certificate draws people who lead across cultural difference, whether that difference crosses a national border or sits in the next classroom. You do not need to be in one of these roles now; some students enroll to move into this kind of work.

People you serve come from different places.

You run a school, a clinic, or a department where families and staff bring different languages and expectations into the same building. Much of what you know you learned on the job. This certificate gives you formal preparation.

01Public schools · Hospitals · Universities · Social services

You lead people you rarely meet.

Your colleagues start their day as yours ends. You work with them through screens and written messages, and you may never share a room with the people you depend on. You want to know how to maintain trust and keep decisions moving under those conditions.

02Remote-first companies · Multi-site organizations · Distributed teams

Your decisions affect other countries.

Your students, patients, or partners live in places you may never visit. You decide about conditions you have not seen firsthand. You want a better understanding of the people those decisions affect.

03Foreign service · International development · Global trade · Education abroad
Coursework

Four courses, twelve credits.

Four courses, one per term, with most students finishing in twelve to sixteen months. All four are anchored in Cultural Intelligence (CQ), a research-based framework with four capabilities: Drive, Knowledge, Strategy, and Action. Each course develops specific capabilities and produces a document for your portfolio.

ITL 6100

Cross-Cultural Communication in Global Leadership

Foundations of cross-cultural communication, using the GLOBE dimensions and Meyer's cultural scales. You profile your own cultural positioning, analyze a real communication case, and apply the frameworks in an intercultural encounter. Introduces CQ Drive and CQ Knowledge.

3 cr.
ITL 6200

Technology for International Collaborations

The cultural assumptions inside collaboration platforms, and the limits of AI as a cultural translator. You audit the tools you use, test AI against a real cross-cultural message, and produce a virtual collaboration playbook. Develops CQ Strategy.

3 cr.
ITL 6300

Leadership for Global Challenges

Leadership on problems that cross cultural lines, from public health and migration to a school district with a growing English-learner population. You choose one, diagnose it, and propose a response that accounts for culture. Deepens CQ Knowledge.

3 cr.
ITL 6400

Leadership for International Teams

Recruiting, motivating, and aligning teams across language and culture, including teams whose members never meet in person. You work through cases and simulations, and take a conflict situation through to a decision. Develops CQ Action.

3 cr.
How the courses are structured

All four courses follow the same structure.

The subject matter changes from course to course. The sequence does not. Each course moves from assessment through application to a completed piece of professional work.

01
Assessment

You begin by establishing where you stand.

The opening weeks establish a baseline. ITL 6100 uses research instruments to profile your cultural positioning. The remaining courses assess the technologies, teams, and problems that define your professional context.

  • Cultural profilecomplete
  • Assumptionsidentify
  • Baselinedocument
  • Development goalsdefine
02
Application

You apply the frameworks to documented cases.

The middle units apply the frameworks to real professional situations. You locate examples in current practice, publish your analysis for the group, and respond to the analyses your classmates submit.

  • Professional casesanalyze
  • Frameworksapply
  • Written analysispublish
  • Peer responsecontribute
03
Production

You produce a document for your portfolio.

The final unit is devoted to the signature project. You develop it across several weeks, revise it against the project rubric, and submit it as finished professional work.

  • Signature projectdevelop
  • Project rubricapply
  • Final documentsubmit

What you finish with

Four documents, one from each course.

Every course ends with a signature project. The four documents make up your portfolio, and they are written for readers outside the program, so they are ready to share with a hiring committee or a supervisor.

One document per course

Your portfolio is what employers can read.

Your transcript lists the courses you took. Your portfolio shows what you produced in them. You keep the documents after you graduate and revise them as your work changes.

01
From ITL 6100

Personal Cross-Cultural Communication Guide

Your own cultural profile, the frameworks you use to read other people's, and the strategies you will apply in your next intercultural encounter.

02
From ITL 6200

Cross-Cultural Virtual Collaboration Playbook

Platform recommendations, a communication strategy for working across distance, and an assessment of where AI helps and where it fails.

03
From ITL 6300

Global challenge analysis

A problem that crosses cultural lines, diagnosed with research, with a proposed response that accounts for culture.

04
From ITL 6400

Team leadership case

A team situation across language and culture, analyzed through to a decision and the reasoning behind it.

Common questions

Questions we are asked most often.

If your question does not appear here, contact the program director. We will respond directly.

Do I need to work internationally to enroll?

No. The certificate is designed for anyone who leads across cultural difference, whether that difference crosses a national border or exists within a single institution. School administrators, healthcare leaders, and managers of distributed teams enroll alongside those working abroad.

Do I need a current employer?

No. Coursework can be completed using a current organization, a former one, or a documented case. Students who enroll while changing fields complete the same assignments and produce the same portfolio.

Are there scheduled online sessions?

Most coursework is asynchronous and can be completed on your own schedule within posted deadlines. Some courses include scheduled online sessions for collaborative work. Any required sessions are identified in the course schedule.

Is travel required?

No. All four courses are delivered fully online.

Is travel available?

The program has offered short international experiences in the past. None is required to earn the certificate, and none is guaranteed in a given year.

How long does the certificate take?

Most students complete the four courses in twelve to sixteen months, taking one course per term. You may take more than one course at a time or pause between terms.

Does the certificate apply toward a master's or doctorate?

Credits earned in the certificate can be applied toward several App State master's programs in related fields. The courses may also be taken individually within the interdisciplinary Ed.D.

What does it cost?

App State graduate tuition rates apply, and the program is eligible for federal financial aid. If your employer requires documentation to support tuition assistance, the program director can provide it.

What technology is required?

A reliable computer, a webcam and microphone, and broadband internet access. The university provides access to all course platforms.

Admissions

How to apply.

The application process has four steps.

The path

  1. Contact the program director
    Email the program director with your professional goals and your current context. Questions about fit, coursework, and timing are best addressed before you begin the application.
  2. Submit the application
    Complete the App State Graduate School online application. It requires transcripts, a current résumé, and a short statement of professional purpose. The GRE is not required.
  3. Provide two recommendations
    One professional and one academic recommendation is preferred. Recommenders should address your professional work and your capacity for graduate study.
  4. Receive a decision
    Applications are reviewed as they are received. Submit with sufficient lead time before your intended start term.
